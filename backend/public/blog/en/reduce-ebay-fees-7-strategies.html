How to Reduce Your eBay Fees:
7 Concrete Strategies (2026)

Every eBay sale costs you 10–17%. Here are 7 concrete levers to take back control of your margin.

On eBay, every sale costs you between 10% and 17% of its price: the final value fee (8–15% depending on category) plus payment processing fees. On €40,000 of annual sales, that's €4,000–7,000 gone every year.

Good news: these fees are not set in stone. Here are 7 concrete strategies to cut them, ordered from simplest to most advanced.

1. Choose the right store subscription

eBay offers several subscription tiers. If you sell regularly, a store subscription lowers your insertion fees and unlocks selling tools. Compare the subscription cost with the insertion fees you save: beyond a certain sales volume, the subscription quickly pays for itself.

2. Check the category of every listing

The final value fee depends directly on the category you list in. Some categories have much lower rates than others. A misplaced item can cost you several points of margin. Check the applied rate before every listing, and if your product can legitimately belong to two categories, pick the cheaper one.

3. Master Promoted Listings

Promoted listings boost your sales, but they take an extra percentage on every sale they generate. The trap: paying for ads on products that already rank well or have thin margins. Only promote high-margin products, and measure the real ad cost before scaling up.

4. Optimize your shipping costs

Shipping is where many sellers lose money without realizing it. Compare carriers, set realistic shipping charges, and avoid under-pricing postage. Every euro lost on shipping is a euro of margin gone.

5. Avoid disputes and returns

An unresolved dispute can trigger fees and hurt your visibility. Clear photos, accurate descriptions, and fast communication drastically reduce returns and complaints. Prevention always costs less than a dispute.

6. Aim for Top Rated Seller status

Top-rated sellers get concrete advantages: better placement in results, stronger protections, and sometimes fee discounts. Take care of your shipping times, descriptions, and customer service to reach that status.

7. Measure before you cut — the key to it all

You can only reduce what you measure. Most sellers have only a vague idea of their real fees: commissions, payments, promotions, subscriptions...
