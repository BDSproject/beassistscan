Leaving eBay:
The Guide to Migrating to Your Own Store

Take back control of your margins, your customers, and your brand — without losing sales along the way.

You've been selling on eBay for years, and you're tired of watching 10–17% of every sale go to fees. You dream of your own store, with your own customers, margins, and brand. Good news: it's possible, and more and more sellers are making the move.

When should you leave eBay?

There's no magic number, but a good benchmark: above €15,000 in annual sales, the fees you pay eBay are enough to fully fund your own store (hosting + platform). Below that, eBay remains an excellent launchpad.

The alternatives to eBay

  • Shopify: the most accessible way to launch a store in a few days (~€30/month).
  • WooCommerce (WordPress): ideal if you already have a website, very flexible and open-source.
  • PrestaShop: a solid open-source alternative for more advanced needs.

The steps to a successful migration

  • Export your catalog from eBay (titles, descriptions, photos, prices).
  • Choose a domain name and set up your store.
  • Set up payments (Stripe, PayPal) and logistics.
  • Optimize SEO to attract Google traffic without depending on eBay.
  • Gradually redirect your eBay customers to your store (through allowed channels).

The hybrid strategy: don't cut everything overnight

The classic trap is leaving everything at once and losing your income. The right approach: keep eBay as an acquisition channel while your store grows, then gradually reduce your dependency. You diversify your revenue and secure your transition.

❓ Frequently asked questions

At what revenue should I leave eBay?

A good benchmark is €15,000 in annual sales: at that level, the fees you pay fully fund your own store.

What is the best alternative to eBay?

Shopify for simplicity, WooCommerce if you already have a WordPress site, PrestaShop for advanced needs. It depends on your profile.

Can I keep eBay alongside my store?

Yes, it's even recommended: use eBay for acquisition and your store to retain customers and improve margins.
